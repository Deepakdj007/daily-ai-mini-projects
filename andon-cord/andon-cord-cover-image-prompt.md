# andon-cord — cover image

16:9 landscape. One metaphor: the hand that decides whether to stop everything.

## Primary prompt

> Extreme close-up, cinematic, of a single human hand reaching up into frame and
> closing around a worn yellow pull-cord that hangs from an unseen ceiling in a
> dark industrial aisle. The cord is frayed at the grip from years of use, its
> yellow gone dull and greasy. The hand is mid-decision, fingers just touching
> the cord, not yet pulling.
>
> One hard shaft of light falls from high and far behind, raking across the
> knuckles and catching the cord, leaving everything beyond the hand in deep
> falloff. Long machine silhouettes recede into blackness behind, suggested only,
> never described. Shallow depth of field, the cord sharp, the background dissolved.
>
> Dust hangs in the light beam. Muted palette, warm amber on the cord against
> cold near-black steel. Photographic, shot on a 85mm lens, high contrast, heavy
> shadow, no fill light. Grain of real film.
>

## Alternate, if the hand reads too passive

> Extreme close-up of a single yellow pull-cord hanging dead centre in a dark
> factory aisle, still swinging, slightly blurred at the bottom of its arc from
> having just been pulled. Nobody in frame. One hard directional light from the
> upper left catches the cord and nothing else. Deep black falloff on all sides,
> machinery only implied. Warm amber against cold steel, shallow depth of field,
> heavy grain, high contrast, no fill light. Photographic, 85mm.
>
> --ar 16:9 --style raw --v 7

## Notes

The emotional beat is restraint, not alarm. The cord is old and used, so the
viewer reads a habit of judgement rather than an emergency. Keep the hand or the
cord as the single subject: no control panels, no screens, no workers in the
background, no sparks. The light does all the work.
