# understudy — cover image prompt

## The idea

An engineer in a workshop, mid-adjustment, and the machine on the bench is
copying him perfectly — same tilt of the head, same set of the shoulders — with
nothing behind its face. The mimicry is flawless and hollow. That is the
project: it learned his delivery and none of his memories.

The robot must read as *well made and empty*, not as *sci-fi and alive*. No
glowing eyes. The moment the machine looks like it is thinking, the image is
saying the opposite of what the guide says.

## Midjourney / Flux / DALL·E prompt

> Wide cinematic shot inside a real working repair shop, late afternoon. A
> lone engineer in a worn canvas apron leans over a cluttered steel workbench,
> one hand resting on the shoulder panel of a seated humanoid robot, a small
> driver in the other hand, caught mid-adjustment. The robot is plainly
> machined — brushed aluminium and matte grey composite, visible joints, honest
> seams, scuffed from use — and it is mirroring the engineer's posture exactly:
> the same lean, the same tilt of the head, the same angle of the shoulders.
> Its faceplate is smooth and featureless, unlit, giving nothing back. The
> engineer's face is lit and alive with concentration. Single hard directional
> light from a tall dusty window at camera left, raking across both figures,
> warm sunlight against cool shadow, fine dust hanging in the beam. Background
> falls off into a dim clutter of hand tools, cable spools and parts bins.
> Shot on 35mm, shallow depth of field, natural film grain, visible skin and
> metal texture, no retouching. Muted palette — warm tungsten and daylight,
> steel grey, oxidised red, no other colour. Documentary stillness, not drama.
> --ar 16:9 --style raw --v 7

## Variant — the reflection

Tighter, and the single strongest version of the idea if you want one frame to
carry it.

> Extreme close-up of a humanoid robot's blank faceplate on a workbench, warm
> late-afternoon light raking across it from one side. The faceplate is smooth
> brushed metal with no eyes and no features, and in its polished surface the
> engineer's own face is reflected, leaning in close, lit and concentrating —
> the only face the machine has. Fine scratches and fingerprints in the metal.
> Shot on 85mm, shallow depth of field, the workshop behind dissolving into
> warm shadow. Natural film grain, real metal texture. Warm daylight, steel
> grey and deep shadow only. Quiet, documentary, unsentimental.
> --ar 16:9 --style raw --v 7

## Variant — hands only

> Extreme close-up of two pairs of hands on a scarred steel workbench in warm
> raking window light: a human hand mid-gesture, and a machined aluminium hand
> beside it caught in exactly the same gesture, joint for joint. The metal hand
> is well made and slightly worn, matte, with no lights anywhere on it. Shallow
> depth of field, 85mm, background falling into dim workshop clutter. Film
> grain, real skin and metal texture, warm daylight against cool shadow, no
> other colour. --ar 16:9 --style raw --v 7

## Why this one

The copying is the subject. An engineer and a robot in a workshop is a stock
image; an engineer and a robot holding the *same posture* is the guide's
argument in one frame.

The light does the emotional work and it only falls on one of them. The
engineer is warm and readable. The machine is lit by the same sun and gives
nothing back, because there is nothing in there to light.

**Keep out of the frame:** glowing eyes, LED strips, holograms, floating UI
panels, HUD overlays, neon of any colour, blue rim lighting, atmospheric haze
or smoke, a network diagram on a screen, a laptop showing code. Every one of
those makes the machine look like it is thinking. The whole point is that it
is not.
